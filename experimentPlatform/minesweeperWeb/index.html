Minesweeper Experiment

Experiment Debriefing

This website is a research tool being used to collect data on humans playing Minesweeper, in an attempt to investigate the effective difficulty of Minesweeper fields, and how this changes across players.
To achieve this, we present you with a classic game of Minesweeper, which we've modified to internally record the way you solve a field.

In the recording of a field solve, we're specifically interested in the difficulty of moves you make, where a move's "difficulty" corresponds to the amount of information that would be necessary to make that move.
In theory, move "difficulty" can be determined by constraint programming techniques, namely relational arc consistency.
Move difficulty may then be used to identify difficult portions of a Minesweeper field, and subsequently help to understand the skill composition of a player by looking at what they do upon encountering areas of different difficulty.

If you have any questions or comments about the experiment, or want to discuss anything, you're welcome to contact the student responsible for this, Andrei Boghean, at 2645295b@student.gla.ac.uk


Participation Disclaimers

This experiment explicitly excludes participants under 16, or participants who possess any impairments that may limit their understanding or ability to communicate.
If you are such a person, please do not participate in this experiment. If you are looking to play Minesweeper, we suggest other websites such as minesweeper.online

We also want to highlight that this is not a test of skill. For the best results, we welcome participants with a wide variety of ability.
Further, you are under no obligation to participate in or complete this experiment, and have the right to withdraw at any time if you so desire.

Minesweeper Instructions and Controls

Rules:

In Minesweeper, the primary goal is to uncover all the safe cells, while not uncovering any mines in the process.
Upon uncovering a cell, the cell reveals the number of mines within the 8 neighbouring cells around it.
This information can be used to deduce additional safe cells, and ultimately complete the field.
Uncovering a mine of course will lose the game and stop the timer.

Cells suspected to contain a mine can be flagged, and a cell whose flagged neighbours equal its number of neighbouring mines will allow you to "chord" it, revealing all neighbouring unflagged cells in one action.
additionally, any cells you discover which have a hint of 0, i.e. have no neighbouring mines, will have their neighbours automatically uncovered.


Controls:

Left-click or tap a closed cell to open the cell.
Right-click or long-press a closed cell to flag the cell.
Right-click or long-press a revealed cell with a number to carry out a chord.
Left-click or tap the smiley face to restart with a new, random, game.

Privacy Disclaimers

Data Intake

Tthis experiment looks at and stores the following information, for the following purposes:

Stores: your playthrough data (including type of move made, on what field cell, at what time).
In order to: analyse, using algorithms being researched, as part of the goal to understand how player abilities vary with skill.

Stores: a unique private playerID generated from the time you open the website, and a second public playerID which you can arbitrarily set.
In order to: group playthroughs by the same player (not for personal identification).
(Note that these IDs are attached to your browser via local storage.)

Stores: the user-agent string your browser reports.
In order to: have an indicator of whether you're playing on desktop or mobile.
