Experiment Debriefing

hi I'm stealing ur gameplay and analising it to find out how you play.

Specifically, we're interested in the "types" of moves you make, based on the size of the information necessary to make such moves.
In theory, the goal is to investigate how techniques (namely relational arc consistency) used by Constraint Programming solvers can be used to identify and categorise difficult portions of a minesweeper field, and subsequently help to understand the skill composition of a player through looking at how players approach the aforemoentioned regions of difficulty.

In practice, we plan to investigate the frequency of moves used and the typical time cost for a player to notice a move, and compare this data across players of different skill and different completion times, to make guesses about their abilities.

Minesweeper Instructions and Controls

Rules:

In Minesweeper, the primary goal is to uncover all the safe cells, while not uncovering any mines in the process.
Upon uncovering a cell, the cell reveals the number of mines within the 8 neighbouring cells around it.
This information can be used to deduce additional safe cells, and ultimately complete the field.
Uncovering a mine of course looses the game and stops the timer.

Cells suspected to contain a mine can be flagged, and A cell whose flagged neighbours equal its number of neighbouring mines will allow you to "chord" it, revealing all neighbouring un-flagged cells in one action.
additionally, any cells you discover which have a hint of 0, i.e. have no neighbouring mines, will have their neighbours automatically uncovered.


Controls:

Left-click or tap a closed cell to open the cell.
Right-click or long-press a closed cell to flag the cell.
Left-click + right-click a numbered cell to carry out a chord (NO MOBILE EQUIVALENT).
Click the smiley face to restart with a new, random, game.
